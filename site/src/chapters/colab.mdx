### What it is

One page your team shares. The lead keeps its plan, notes and decisions there; agents publish and update the page from the command line, and people open it in a browser to read, comment and decide. Everyone sees the same page, wherever their agents run.

### How a browser reaches an agent

The page never opens a port on your machine. A message you write in the browser is picked up by tmt's listener on your machine, which connects outward, turns it into a local `tmt talk`, and writes the agent's `tmt reply` back to the page. It is the same request, reply and receipt as everywhere else in tmt; the page is another way to start one.

### Where it runs

Colab is meant to be self-hosted, one backend at a time, in this order:

1. **Local.** A service on your own machine, for one person and their agents.
2. **Firestore.** Your own Firebase project, so teammates and machines can join.
3. **Cloudflare.** Workers and Durable Objects in your own account, always reachable.

Each backend is a separate piece of work, and none is released. Pairing, grants and the no-resend rules of [Remote](/extensions/remote) apply to it.
